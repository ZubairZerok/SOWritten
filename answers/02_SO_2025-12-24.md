## Ans. to the Q. No. 1 — The Role of Digital Economy in Economic Development

### Digital Economy: The New Engine of Bangladesh's Development

> "The digital economy is the economy." — Don Tapscott (paraphrased)

**Introduction**
A digital economy is the part of economic activity that runs on digital technology: online payments, e-commerce, mobile financial services, software exports, platform work and digital public services. For a populous, young country like Bangladesh, it can cut transaction costs, include the unbanked and create jobs that do not need large capital.

**Bangladesh's digital economy at a glance**

| Indicator | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| National interoperable QR | "Bangla QR" mandatory; closed-loop QR phased out by 30 June 2026 | Bangladesh Bank, MPS H1FY27 |
| Instant interoperable payments | IIPS targeted by June 2027 (MoU with Gates Foundation) | Bangladesh Bank, MPS H1FY27 |
| Digital nano-loans | Refinance scheme extended to June 2028 | Bangladesh Bank, MPS H1FY27 |
| Financial literacy | 5,531 programmes reached 2.85 lakh people in 2025 | Bangladesh Bank, MPS H1FY27 |

**How the digital economy drives development**
1. **Financial inclusion:** mobile financial services and agent banking bring rural people, women and small traders into the formal system; digital nano-loans give collateral-free credit.
2. **Productivity:** digital payments, e-procurement and online tax filing reduce time, cost and leakage.
3. **Employment:** software firms, IT-enabled services and online freelancing employ hundreds of thousands of young people and earn foreign exchange.
4. **Market access for SMEs:** e-commerce and social commerce let small producers sell nationwide and abroad.
5. **Transparency and governance:** digital trails reduce corruption in public services and social safety-net payments.
6. **Remittances:** digital channels make formal remittance faster and cheaper than hundi.

**Challenges**
- Digital divide between urban and rural areas and between men and women.
- Cyber-attacks and fraud; the 2016 reserve heist remains a warning.
- Weak data-protection law and consumer protection in e-commerce.
- High internet cost and unstable connectivity outside cities.
- Shortage of skilled ICT workforce.

**Role of banks**
Banks must lead with secure digital banking, interoperable payments (Bangla QR, IIPS), cyber-security investment, e-KYC for easy account opening, and credit scoring based on digital transaction data for SMEs.

**Recommendations**
1. Enact and enforce a comprehensive data-protection law.
2. Reduce internet cost and expand rural broadband.
3. Train youth in AI, data analytics and cyber security.
4. Make all government payments and services digital end-to-end.
5. Build a national cyber-security response team for the financial sector.

**Conclusion**
The digital economy can help Bangladesh move from a low-cost manufacturing economy to a knowledge-based, inclusive one. Success depends on trust: secure systems, protected data and digitally skilled people.

## Ans. to the Q. No. 2 — Climate Change and the Productivity of Bangladesh

Tip: Write this in Bangla in the exam. Bangla sub-headings: ভূমিকা · জলবায়ু পরিবর্তনের বাস্তবতা · কৃষি উৎপাদনশীলতায় প্রভাব · শ্রম উৎপাদনশীলতা ও তাপপ্রবাহ · শিল্প ও অবকাঠামো · স্বাস্থ্য ও অভিবাসন · করণীয় (অভিযোজন, সবুজ অর্থায়ন) · উপসংহার

### Climate Change and the Productivity of Bangladesh

> "We are the first generation to feel the effect of climate change and the last generation who can do something about it." — Barack Obama

**Introduction**
Bangladesh contributes very little to global emissions but is one of the countries most exposed to climate change. Rising temperature, erratic rainfall, salinity, floods and cyclones are already reducing the productivity of land, labour and capital.

**Impact on productivity**
1. **Agriculture:** salinity in the coastal belt, untimely rain and flash floods damage Boro and Aman crops. In 2026, crop losses from flash floods added to food prices (Bangladesh Bank, MPS H1FY27).
2. **Labour productivity:** heatwaves reduce working hours for farmers, construction workers and garment workers; heat stress lowers output per hour.
3. **Industry and infrastructure:** floods and waterlogging damage roads, factories and power supply, raising production costs.
4. **Health:** dengue, diarrhoea and heat-related illness reduce workdays.
5. **Migration:** river erosion and salinity push people into urban slums, where they become low-productivity informal workers.

**What needs to be done**
- **Adaptation:** saline- and flood-tolerant rice varieties, raised homesteads, embankments and early-warning systems.
- **Green finance:** Bangladesh Bank's sustainable finance policy should direct more credit to solar irrigation, energy-efficient factories and climate-resilient housing.
- **Heat action plans:** shaded workplaces, flexible working hours and urban greening.
- **Climate diplomacy:** demand fair access to the Loss and Damage Fund and adaptation finance from developed countries.

**Conclusion**
Climate change is not only an environmental issue but a productivity issue. Investing in adaptation today protects tomorrow's growth.

## Ans. to the Q. No. 4 — Reading Comprehension

(a) **Title:** "Schumpeter's Entrepreneur and the Creative Destruction of Capitalism."
(b) Adam Smith saw capitalism as an abstract, faceless force driven by "animal spirit". Schumpeter gave capitalism a human face: the entrepreneur, whose innovations drive change.
(c) Economic progress is becoming de-personalised. Change no longer needs an individual leader on the battlefield; organisations and the industrial bourgeoisie are replacing individual entrepreneurs.
(d) Schumpeter believed capitalism would weaken gradually by itself, not through revolution, because invention and innovation have their own limits.
(e) A true entrepreneur breaks old rules: redefining industry boundaries, extending the production possibility curve and creating new markets. Like a religious reformer, he challenges established beliefs and practices.
(f) He used "creative destruction" to describe how new technologies and firms destroy old ones and create a more efficient economic structure, for example power plants replacing water-wheels and airplanes replacing mail coaches.

Relative speed = 75 − 60 = 15 km/h. Time to overtake = 120 ÷ 15 = 8 hours.

**(খ)** Let there be n persons, each getting x taka; total = nx.
(n + 4)(x − 16) = nx ⇒ x − 4n = 16 … (i)
(n − 4)(x + 24) = nx ⇒ 6n − x = 24 … (ii)
Adding: 2n = 40 ⇒ n = 20 (x = Tk 96).
**Answer: 20 persons.**

**(গ)** Commission earned = 10,400 − 5,000 = Tk 5,400.
If sales were ≤ Tk 60,000, maximum commission = 5% of 60,000 = Tk 3,000 < 5,400. So sales > Tk 60,000.
Without bonus: 3,000 + 8% of (S − 60,000) = 5,400 ⇒ S − 60,000 = 30,000 ⇒ S = 90,000 (≤ 1,00,000, so no bonus ✓).
With bonus the result would be S = 77,500, which is below 1,00,000, so that case is impossible.
**Answer: Total sales = Tk 90,000.**
