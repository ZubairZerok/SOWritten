# Practice Set in the June 2026 Format — Questions and Model Answers

> The June 2026 Analytical question had four parts: (a) a logic or scheduling puzzle, (b) critique a decision, (c) find the hidden assumption, (d) arguments for and against. Expect the same four types on 27 November. Both puzzles below have been checked by computer to have exactly one solution.

## Ans. to the Practice Q. No. 6 (Set 1) — Analytical Reasoning (40)

**(a) Question:** Six loan files — P, Q, R, S, T and U — are reviewed one per day from Monday to Saturday. S is reviewed on Wednesday. P is reviewed before S, but not on Monday. T is reviewed after S. Q is reviewed on the day immediately after T. U is reviewed after Q. R is not reviewed on Saturday. Determine the schedule.

**Answer:**
1. S is on Wednesday, and P is before S but not on Monday, so P is on **Tuesday**.
2. T is after S, Q immediately follows T, and U comes after Q. The only three days after Wednesday are Thursday, Friday and Saturday, so **T = Thursday, Q = Friday, U = Saturday**.
3. Only Monday is left, so **R = Monday**. R is not on Saturday ✓.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R | P | S | T | Q | U |

**(b) Question:** A bank's marketing head reports that branches which ran a television advertisement grew deposits by 15 percent last year, while branches without the advertisement grew by only 5 percent. He concludes that the advertisement caused the extra growth and proposes tripling the advertising budget. Critique this decision.

**Answer:** The conclusion confuses correlation with causation.
1. **Selection bias:** the advertised branches may be in cities where income and remittance inflows are already higher.
2. **Other causes:** a higher deposit rate, new products or a new branch manager may explain the growth.
3. **Timing:** a festival season or remittance surge (FY26 remittances +17.3 percent) may have lifted all deposits.
4. **Diminishing returns:** tripling the budget will not triple the effect; the same customers see the same advertisement.
5. **Cost-benefit:** the decision ignores whether the extra deposits are worth the cost of advertising.

**Better approach:** run a controlled test in similar branches, compare like with like, and measure cost per taka of new deposits before scaling up.

**(c) Question:** "Bangladesh Bank has cut the policy rate from 10 percent to 9.5 percent. Private investment will therefore rise, because businesses borrow more when credit is cheaper." What is the implied assumption?

**Answer:** The argument assumes that **the main reason businesses are not investing is the high cost of credit**, and that banks will pass the lower rate on to borrowers. If the real obstacles are energy shortages, political uncertainty or weak banks unwilling to lend, the rate cut will not raise investment. Private credit growth was a record-low 4.17 percent in September 2026, even after the cut.

**(d) Question:** Provide arguments for and against: "Bangladesh should accept a new IMF loan programme even with strict conditions."

**Answer:**
*For:*
1. It provides USD 4.5–5 billion in budget and reserve support at low cost when reserves are under pressure (BPM6 about USD 30 billion).
2. An IMF programme signals credibility and unlocks financing from the World Bank, ADB and Japan.
3. The conditions push reforms that Bangladesh needs anyway: banking clean-up, revenue (tax-GDP only about 7 percent) and a flexible exchange rate.

*Against:*
1. Conditions such as subsidy cuts and higher fuel prices can hurt the poor during high inflation.
2. Reforms imposed from outside may lack national ownership and be reversed.
3. The previous programme (USD 4.7 billion, raised to USD 5.5 billion) stalled, and its targets, such as cutting default loans to 10 percent, were missed. Signing a programme is not the same as reforming.

*Position:* **Accept the programme, but negotiate the sequencing.** Protect social spending, phase energy-price adjustments, and own the reform agenda so that the programme supports national priorities rather than dictating them.

## Ans. to the Practice Q. No. 6 (Set 2) — Analytical Reasoning (40)

**(a) Question:** Five officers — Rafi, Sumi, Tanvir, Nila and Omar — are ranked from 1 (highest sales) to 5. Nila ranks higher than Rafi, and Rafi ranks higher than Sumi. Omar ranks third. Tanvir is neither first nor last. Rafi is not second. Find the ranking.

**Answer:**
1. Omar is 3rd. Tanvir is not 1st or 5th, so Tanvir is 2nd or 4th.
2. Nila > Rafi > Sumi, and Rafi is not 2nd, so Rafi must be 4th (3rd is taken). Then Sumi is 5th.
3. Tanvir must be 2nd (4th is taken), so Nila is 1st.

**Ranking: 1 Nila, 2 Tanvir, 3 Omar, 4 Rafi, 5 Sumi.**

**(b) Question:** After 100 CCTV cameras were installed in a market area, reported crimes rose by 20 percent. A councillor concludes that the cameras increased crime and should be removed. Critique the conclusion.

**Answer:** Cameras increase **detection and reporting**, not necessarily crime. Victims report more when evidence exists, and police record more incidents. Other factors such as population growth, economic hardship or a new reporting system may also explain the rise. The correct measures are conviction rates, serious-crime trends and comparison with similar areas without cameras. Removing the cameras on this evidence would be a mistake.

**(c) Question:** "Mobile financial service accounts in Bangladesh now outnumber adults, so financial inclusion of the poor has been achieved." Which fact would most **weaken** this argument?

**Answer:** The argument is weakened if **many accounts are inactive or duplicates**, for example one person holding several accounts, or accounts used only to receive transfers and immediately cash out. In that case account numbers overstate real inclusion, which means regular saving, borrowing and insurance by poor people. The freezing of more than 80,000 MFS accounts used for hundi and gambling in 2026 shows that account counts alone can mislead.

**(d) Question:** Provide arguments for and against: "Merging weak banks with stronger banks is the best solution to Bangladesh's banking crisis."

**Answer:**
*For:* it protects depositors quickly; achieves economies of scale; and avoids the panic of a bank closure. Sammilito Islami Bank, formed from five banks in 2026, began repaying depositors in September.
*Against:* it can spread bad loans to healthy banks; rewards the owners who looted banks (moral hazard); and does not fix the root cause, which is political lending and weak supervision. The five merged banks had about 86 percent of their Tk 1.92 lakh crore loans in default.
*Position:* **Merger is a tool, not the solution.** It must follow an independent asset-quality review, remove the old owners, bring in new management, and be combined with legal action to recover stolen money.

## Ans. to the Practice Q. No. 9 — Financial and Banking Knowledge (10)

(a) **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio):** the share of a bank's deposits that must be held in liquid assets such as cash, gold and government securities.
(b) **Repo:** a short-term loan from the central bank to a bank against government securities; its rate is the policy rate, now 9.5 percent in Bangladesh.
(c) **NPL (non-performing loan):** a loan whose principal or interest is overdue beyond the classification period (generally 90 days). Bangladesh's ratio was 32.17 percent in September 2026.
(d) **CRAR under Basel III:** the minimum capital to risk-weighted assets ratio of 10 percent plus a 2.5 percent conservation buffer, i.e. 12.5 percent.
(e) **Letter of credit (LC):** a bank's written undertaking to pay the exporter on behalf of the importer when documents conform to the LC terms (governed by UCP 600).
(f) **KYC:** "Know Your Customer", the process of verifying a customer's identity and risk profile when opening and running an account.
(g) **BFIU:** Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit, which receives suspicious transaction reports and fights money laundering and terrorist financing.
(h) **Deposit insurance:** under the Deposit Protection Act 2026, each depositor is protected up to Tk 2 lakh if a bank fails.
(i) **Bancassurance:** selling insurance products through a bank's branches and customer base.
(j) **Expected credit loss (ECL):** IFRS 9 provisioning that requires banks to set aside money for expected future loan losses, not just losses that have already occurred.

## Ans. to the Practice Q. No. 4 — Mathematics (30): Past Senior Officer Questions with Full Solutions

**(a) Sonali Bank SO 2019.** A and B are two stations 390 km apart. A train starts from A at 10 a.m. at 65 km/h towards B; another starts from B at 11 a.m. at 35 km/h towards A. When do they meet?
By 11 a.m. the first train covers 65 km; remaining = 325 km. Relative speed = 65 + 35 = 100 km/h. Time = 3.25 h = 3 h 15 min after 11 a.m.
**Answer: 2:15 p.m.**

**(b) Combined 8 Bank SO 2019.** A can contains liquids A and B in the ratio 7 : 5. When 9 litres of mixture are drawn off and the can is filled with B, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. How much A was there initially?
Let the mixture be 12k litres (A = 7k, B = 5k). The 9 litres drawn contain A = 21/4 and B = 15/4.
(7k − 21/4) : (5k − 15/4 + 9) = 7 : 9
9(7k − 21/4) = 7(5k + 21/4) ⇒ 63k − 189/4 = 35k + 147/4 ⇒ 28k = 84 ⇒ k = 3
**Answer: A = 7 × 3 = 21 litres.**

**(c) Combined 6 Banks & 2 FIs SO 2019.** At a certain compound rate a sum becomes 8 times in 3 years. In how many years will it become 16 times?
(1 + r)³ = 8 = 2³ ⇒ 1 + r = 2. Then (1 + r)ⁿ = 16 = 2⁴ ⇒ **n = 4 years.**

**(d) Combined 6 Banks & 2 FIs SO 2019.** A 15 km road is to be built in 300 days by 45 men. After 100 days, 2.5 km is complete. How many extra men are needed to finish on time?
Man-days per km = (45 × 100) ÷ 2.5 = 1,800. Remaining 12.5 km needs 12.5 × 1,800 = 22,500 man-days in 200 days ⇒ 112.5 men, i.e. 113 men.
**Answer: 113 − 45 = 68 extra men.**

**(e) Rupali Bank SO 2019.** A and B together can do a work in 30 days. A works 16 days, then B finishes the rest in 44 days. How long will B alone take?
30A + 30B = 16A + 44B ⇒ 14A = 14B ⇒ A = B. Together they take 30 days, so each alone takes 60 days.
**Answer: 60 days.**
