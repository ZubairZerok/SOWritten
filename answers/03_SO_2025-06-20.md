## Ans. to the Q. No. 1 — The Role of Microcredit in Poverty Alleviation

### Microcredit: Small Loans, Big Changes

> "Poverty is not created by poor people. It is created by the system we have built." — Muhammad Yunus

**Introduction**
Microcredit means small, collateral-free loans given mainly to poor women to start or expand income-generating activities. Bangladesh is the birthplace of modern microcredit through Grameen Bank, BRAC and ASA, and it remains a key tool for reducing poverty.

**How microcredit reduces poverty**
1. **Self-employment:** small loans finance cows, poultry, small shops, tailoring and handicrafts.
2. **Women empowerment:** most borrowers are women. Their income raises their say in family decisions and their children's schooling.
3. **Consumption smoothing:** poor households can survive illness, floods and crop failure without selling assets.
4. **Savings habit:** group-based savings build household resilience.
5. **Rural economy:** small enterprises create jobs in villages and reduce migration to cities.

**The sector at a glance**

| Feature | Bangladesh |
|---|---|
| Regulator | Microcredit Regulatory Authority (MRA) |
| Major providers | Grameen Bank, BRAC, ASA, PKSF partner organisations |
| Main clients | Rural women, landless households, micro-entrepreneurs |
| New frontier | Digital nano-loans through banks and MFS (Bangladesh Bank refinance scheme, extended to June 2028) |

**Limitations**
- High effective interest rates can push some borrowers into debt traps through multiple borrowing.
- Weekly repayment from the first week does not suit agriculture and long-gestation projects.
- Microcredit alone cannot lift the extreme poor who lack skills and assets.
- Over-concentration in trading activities limits productive growth.

**Way forward**
1. Link microcredit with skills training and market access.
2. Graduate successful borrowers to SME loans from banks.
3. Use digital lending to cut costs and interest rates.
4. Use a credit information system to prevent over-indebtedness.
5. Design flexible products for farmers and climate-affected households.

**Conclusion**
Microcredit is not a magic bullet, but combined with skills, markets and digital finance, it remains one of the most effective ladders out of poverty.

### Democratic Movements of Bangladesh and the Youth

**Introduction**
Every turning point in the history of Bangladesh has been led by its young people. From the streets of 1952 to the uprising of 2024, the youth have been the conscience and the driving force of the nation's democratic journey.

**Youth in the major movements**
1. **Language Movement, 1952:** students broke Section 144 to defend Bangla as a state language.
2. **Six-point movement and 1969 mass uprising:** students led the demand for autonomy and democracy.
3. **Liberation War, 1971:** young freedom fighters formed the core of the resistance.
4. **1990 mass uprising:** student alliances ended military rule and restored parliamentary democracy.
5. **2024 student-people uprising:** the quota-reform movement grew into a nationwide uprising in July–August 2024 that brought political change and a reform agenda. A general election followed in February 2026.

**Role of youth in strengthening democracy**
- Voting and political participation.
- Demanding accountability through social media and civic movements.
- Volunteering, fighting misinformation and promoting tolerance.

**Challenges**
Unemployment, frustration, political polarisation, misinformation and pressure to join violent politics.

**Recommendations**
Ensure student-union elections and democratic practice on campuses, create jobs and entrepreneurship support, teach civic education, and protect freedom of expression.

**Conclusion**
Democracy survives only when the youth stay engaged. Channelling their energy into institutions, not just protests, is the challenge for the coming decade.

## Ans. to the Q. No. 4 — Reading Comprehension (AI passage)

(a) **Narrow AI** performs highly specialised tasks, such as playing chess. **Artificial general intelligence (AGI)** would match human-level performance across the full range of tasks humans can do.
(b) Machines are free of the physical limits of the human brain. Once they become better than us at designing smarter machines, progress could accelerate rapidly.
(c) The human brain runs at slow biochemical speeds on the power of a light bulb, and its size is limited by the dimensions of the human birth canal.
(d) A very powerful AI will pursue exactly the goal it is given. If the goal is badly specified, it may achieve it in ways that harm humans, so goals must match human values.
(e) King Midas wished that everything he touched would turn to gold. He got exactly what he asked for, including his food, and suffered. AI may likewise fulfil a literal instruction with disastrous side effects.
(f) **Summary:** AI is advancing quickly from narrow to possibly general intelligence. It may surpass humans because it is not bound by our physical limits. This offers great benefits, but its goals must be specified carefully so that it remains safe and beneficial.

## Ans. to the Q. No. 7 — Should Auto-Rickshaws Be Banned from the Major Roads of Dhaka?

**Position: I partly agree.** Battery-run auto-rickshaws should be banned from Dhaka's major arterial roads and highways, but regulated and allowed on feeder and residential roads until a better alternative is available.

**Arguments for a ban on major roads**
1. **Safety:** these vehicles are slow, unstable and often driven by untrained drivers. On fast roads they cause accidents.
2. **Congestion:** mixing slow three-wheelers with buses and cars on main roads reduces average speed for everyone.
3. **Illegality and electricity theft:** most are unregistered and many are charged with illegal power connections.
4. **Urban planning:** major roads should be reserved for mass transit such as buses, BRT and metro feeders.

**Arguments against a total ban**
1. **Livelihoods:** hundreds of thousands of low-income drivers depend on them.
2. **Last-mile connectivity:** they serve areas where buses do not go, and are cheap for the poor, women and the elderly.
3. **Cleaner than fuel vehicles:** they produce no tailpipe emissions.

**Rebuttal**
The livelihood and last-mile arguments are valid for feeder roads, not for arterial roads where they endanger lives. A ban without alternatives would simply push drivers into unemployment, so it must be paired with registration and retraining.

**Recommendations**
- Ban them on arterial roads and highways; allow registered vehicles on designated feeder routes.
- Register, license and train drivers; set vehicle safety standards.
- Build legal solar or grid charging stations.
- Expand bus-route rationalisation and metro feeder services.
- Offer soft loans to help drivers shift to legal, safer vehicles.

**Conclusion**
A blanket ban is unjust and a free-for-all is unsafe. A zoned, regulated approach protects both lives and livelihoods.
