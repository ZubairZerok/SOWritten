# Financial & Banking Knowledge — 10 Marks Full-Marks Pack (SO, 27 Nov 2026)

## 1. Verdict and the answer formula

**What the paper asks.** The question is Q9 in the 5 June 2026 blueprint: five short items × 2 marks, answered in English. The items fall into four types:

| Type | June 2026 example | How to answer (2 marks) |
|---|---|---|
| **Full form** | SOFR, SWIFT | Write the full form, then add one line saying what it is. |
| **Define in one sentence** | CRR, fiscal policy | "X is the … that …" plus one key detail (who sets it, the current rate). |
| **Name the term** | Securitisation | "The process is called **securitisation**", plus one line of explanation. |
| **Current fact** | (expected) policy rate, NPL, governor | State the number with its date and source: "9.50% (since 2 Aug 2026, Bangladesh Bank)". |

**The formula: one full sentence, the keyword underlined, one value-adding detail.**

- Example. *Q: Define CRR.* → "**Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** is the percentage of a bank's total demand and time liabilities that it must keep as cash with the central bank. In Bangladesh it is set by Bangladesh Bank (4% on a bi-weekly average basis) and is a tool of monetary policy."
- Never answer with a single word. Spell full forms exactly: "Telecommunication" is singular in SWIFT.
- A long BIBM-style stem often carries context you do not need. Answer only the last line.
- Do not guess a number you are unsure of. Write the concept and the direction instead, e.g. "NPL is above 30% of total loans (Sept 2026)".

> **Honest note.** No pack can promise 10/10, because the examiner may ask something new. This pack covers every item type and every topic seen in SO, BB and BIBM-set papers, 2021–2026. It also includes the Bangladesh Bank figures as at 20 Nov 2026 (from the repo's verified Fact Pack). **Realistic target: 8–10.**

## 2. Past questions with model answers

### 2.1 SO 5 June 2026, Q9 (the blueprint)

- **(a) SOFR** = **Secured Overnight Financing Rate**. It is the benchmark US-dollar overnight interest rate, based on Treasury repo transactions and published by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. It replaced **LIBOR**, whose USD panel ended on 30 June 2023.
- **(b) SWIFT** = **Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication**. It is a Belgium-based (La Hulpe), member-owned cooperative, founded in 1973, that provides the secure messaging network banks use for cross-border payments.
- **(c) CRR** is the portion of a bank's deposits (demand and time liabilities) that it must keep as cash with the central bank. Bangladesh Bank uses it to control liquidity and money supply.
- **(d) Fiscal policy** is the government's use of taxation, public spending and borrowing to influence the economy. In Bangladesh the Ministry of Finance sets it through the national budget.
- **(e) Securitisation** is the process in which a bank pools its loans, sells them to a third party (a special purpose vehicle) and the SPV converts their cash flows into tradable securities.

## 4. One-sentence definitions (the "Define …" item type)

### 4.1 Monetary policy and the central bank

- **Monetary policy:** the central bank's management of money supply and interest rates to control inflation and support growth. In Bangladesh it is set by Bangladesh Bank through the MPS.
- **Fiscal policy:** the government's use of taxes, public spending and borrowing to influence the economy, mainly through the annual budget.
- **Policy rate (repo rate):** the rate at which the central bank lends overnight to banks against government securities. It signals the monetary stance (BD: 9.50% since 2 Aug 2026).
- **Reverse repo:** the opposite operation, in which banks park surplus funds with the central bank against securities. It absorbs liquidity.
- **Bank rate:** the rate at which the central bank rediscounts eligible bills of commercial banks. In BD it is a long-standing reference rate.
- **CRR:** the share of deposits a bank must keep as cash with the central bank.
- **SLR:** the share of deposits a bank must keep in liquid assets such as cash, gold and government securities.
- **Open market operations:** the buying and selling of government securities by the central bank to inject or absorb liquidity.
- **Interest rate corridor:** the band between the standing deposit rate (floor) and the standing lending rate (ceiling) within which interbank rates move.
- **Inflation:** a sustained rise in the general price level, measured in BD by the BBS Consumer Price Index.
- **Deflation:** a sustained fall in the general price level.
- **Disinflation:** a fall in the rate of inflation, while prices still rise.
- **Stagflation:** high inflation combined with stagnant growth and high unemployment.
- **Core inflation:** inflation excluding volatile food and energy prices.
- **Quantitative easing:** large-scale asset purchases by a central bank to push down long-term rates when policy rates are near zero.
- **Crowding out:** heavy government borrowing from banks reducing the credit available to the private sector.
- **Liquidity trap:** a situation where interest-rate cuts fail to raise spending because people prefer to hold cash.
- **Money multiplier:** the ratio of broad money to reserve money, showing how bank lending expands the money supply.
- **Reserve money (high-powered money):** currency in circulation plus banks' reserves with the central bank.
- **Broad money (M2):** currency with the public plus demand and time deposits of banks.
- **Lender of last resort:** the central bank's role of giving emergency liquidity to solvent banks in a crisis.
- **Seigniorage:** the profit a government earns by issuing currency (face value minus production cost).
- **Dollarisation:** the use of a foreign currency, usually the US dollar, alongside or instead of the local one.

### 4.2 Banking operations and lending

- **Securitisation:** pooling loans and converting their cash flows into tradable securities sold to investors.
- **Non-performing loan:** a loan whose principal or interest is overdue beyond the regulatory limit, now 90 days in BD.
- **Classified loan:** a loan placed in the Sub-standard, Doubtful or Bad/Loss category under BB's classification rules.
- **Provision:** money a bank sets aside from profit to cover expected loan losses. The traditional BD rates are 1% general, 20% Sub-standard, 50% Doubtful and 100% Bad/Loss.
- **Write-off:** removing an unrecoverable, fully provisioned loan from the balance sheet while recovery efforts continue.
- **Rescheduling:** changing a loan's repayment schedule, usually after a down payment, to give the borrower more time.
- **Wilful defaulter:** a borrower who can repay but deliberately does not.
- **Loan syndication:** a large loan provided jointly by a group of banks under one lead arranger.
- **Overdraft:** a facility to withdraw more than the account balance up to an approved limit.
- **Cash credit:** a running loan account secured by stock or goods (hypothecation or pledge).
- **Hypothecation:** a charge on movable goods where possession stays with the borrower.
- **Pledge:** a charge where the goods are delivered to the bank's possession.
- **Mortgage:** the transfer of an interest in immovable property as security for a loan.
- **Lien:** a bank's right to retain the customer's property until a debt is paid.
- **Collateral:** an asset pledged by the borrower to secure a loan.
- **Bancassurance:** the sale of insurance products through a bank's branches and channels (allowed in BD since 2023).
- **Agent banking:** providing limited banking services through appointed agents outside branches. The first was Bank Asia in 2014.
- **Digital bank:** a bank without physical branches that serves customers fully online (BB guideline 2023).
- **Offshore banking unit (OBU):** a bank unit that deals in foreign currency with non-residents and export-oriented firms.
- **Universal banking:** one bank offering commercial, investment and insurance services.
- **Narrow banking:** a bank that keeps deposits in safe liquid assets only.
- **Shadow banking:** credit intermediation by non-bank entities outside regular bank regulation.
- **Bad bank / asset management company:** an entity set up to buy and manage a bank's toxic assets.
- **Bail-in:** rescuing a failing bank by converting creditors' or shareholders' claims into equity, instead of using taxpayers' money (the bailout).
- **Bank run:** many depositors withdrawing at once because they fear the bank will fail.
- **Deposit insurance:** protection of depositors up to a limit if a bank fails. In BD the limit is Tk 2 lakh under the Deposit Protection Act 2026.
- **Bank resolution:** the orderly restructuring, merger or closure of a failing bank by the authorities (Bank Resolution Act 2026).
- **Moral hazard:** taking excessive risk because someone else (government or insurer) bears the loss.
- **Adverse selection:** riskier borrowers being more likely to seek loans because of information asymmetry.
- **Financial inclusion:** access for all individuals and businesses to affordable formal financial services.
- **Green banking:** financing environment-friendly projects and reducing a bank's own carbon footprint.
- **Basis point:** one-hundredth of one percent (0.01%). A cut from 10% to 9.5% is 50 bps.
- **Spread:** the gap between a bank's lending rate and its deposit rate.
- **Net interest margin:** net interest income divided by average interest-earning assets.
- **Yield curve:** a graph of interest rates on bonds of the same quality across maturities. An inverted curve often signals recession.

### 4.3 Instruments, negotiable instruments and documents

- **Cheque:** a bill of exchange drawn on a specified banker and payable on demand (NI Act 1881, s.6).
- **Crossed cheque:** a cheque with two parallel lines that can only be paid into a bank account, not in cash.
- **Account payee cheque:** a cheque that can be credited only to the named payee's account.
- **Stale cheque:** a cheque presented after its validity period, six months in BD.
- **Post-dated cheque:** a cheque bearing a future date; it cannot be paid before that date.
- **Dishonoured cheque:** a cheque returned unpaid. Under section 138 of the NI Act it is a criminal offence when the account has insufficient funds.
- **Bill of exchange:** an unconditional written order by one person to another to pay a sum to a third person.
- **Promissory note:** an unconditional written promise by the maker to pay a sum to a person.
- **Pay order / demand draft:** a bank-issued instrument guaranteeing payment, payable locally (pay order) or at another branch (DD).
- **Bank guarantee:** the bank's undertaking to pay a beneficiary if its customer fails to perform.
- **Letter of credit:** a bank's written undertaking to pay the seller against documents that comply with the LC terms.
- **Confirmed LC:** an LC to which a second bank adds its own payment undertaking.
- **Revolving LC:** an LC whose amount is restored after use, for repeated shipments.
- **Red clause LC:** an LC allowing an advance to the exporter before shipment.
- **Usance LC (deferred):** an LC payable at a future date after presentation. A **sight LC** is payable on presentation.
- **Bill of lading:** a document issued by the carrier that is both a receipt for goods and a document of title.
- **Nostro account:** our bank's account in foreign currency with a bank abroad. **Vostro** is a foreign bank's account with us.
- **Forward contract:** an agreement to buy or sell foreign currency at a fixed rate on a future date.
- **Swap:** an exchange of cash flows or currencies between two parties over a period.
- **Derivative:** a contract whose value derives from an underlying asset (forward, future, option, swap).
- **Bond:** a debt security in which the issuer promises periodic interest and repayment of principal.
- **Sukuk:** a Shariah-compliant asset-backed investment certificate, used instead of interest-bearing bonds.
- **Treasury bill:** a short-term government security (up to one year) sold at a discount.
- **Commercial paper:** a short-term unsecured promissory note issued by companies.
- **Junk bond:** a high-yield bond below investment grade.
- **Green bond:** a bond whose proceeds fund climate or environmental projects.

### 4.4 Capital market

- **IPO:** the first sale of a company's shares to the public.
- **Bull market / bear market:** a period of rising / falling share prices.
- **Circuit breaker:** a temporary trading halt or price limit triggered by a large price move.
- **Insider trading:** trading on unpublished price-sensitive information, which is illegal.
- **Dividend:** the share of profit distributed to shareholders, as cash or bonus (stock) dividend.
- **Right share:** new shares offered to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings.
- **Market capitalisation:** share price × number of shares outstanding.
- **Blue-chip:** a share of a large, financially sound, well-known company.
- **Mutual fund:** a pool of investors' money managed professionally in a diversified portfolio.
- **Book building:** price discovery for an IPO through bids from institutional investors.
- **Margin loan:** credit given by a broker to an investor to buy shares.

## 7. Regulation: Basel, CAMELS, loan classification

- **Basel I (1988):** credit-risk capital, minimum 8% of risk-weighted assets.
- **Basel II (2004):** three pillars: minimum capital, supervisory review and market discipline. Bangladesh adopted it in 2010.
- **Basel III (2010):** better-quality capital (CET1 4.5%), buffers (conservation 2.5%, countercyclical 0–2.5%), a leverage ratio (3% minimum) and liquidity ratios (LCR, NSFR). BD's roadmap started in 2015.
- **Tiers of capital:** Tier 1 is going-concern capital (paid-up capital, reserves, retained earnings). Tier 2 is gone-concern capital (subordinated debt, general provisions).
- **CAMELS:** Capital adequacy, Asset quality, Management, Earnings, Liquidity, Sensitivity to market risk. Ratings run from 1 (strong) to 5 (critical).
- **BD loan classification:** Standard → SMA → Sub-standard (SS) → Doubtful (DF) → Bad/Loss (BL). SS, DF and BL are classified loans.
- **Three lines of defence:** business units, risk management and compliance, internal audit.
- **Six core risks (BB guideline):** credit, asset-liability, foreign exchange, money laundering, internal control and compliance, ICT.

## 8. Islamic banking

| Mode | Meaning | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Mudaraba** | One party gives capital (*rab-ul-mal*), the other gives labour (*mudarib*). Profit is shared by ratio; the capital provider bears the loss. | Profit-sharing |
| **Musharaka** | Both partners give capital. Profit is shared by agreement, loss by capital ratio. | Partnership |
| **Diminishing Musharaka** | The customer buys out the bank's share gradually (used for home finance). | Partnership |
| **Murabaha** | The bank buys goods and sells them to the client at cost plus a disclosed profit. | Trade |
| **Bai-Muajjal** | A sale with deferred payment (lump sum or instalments). | Trade |
| **Bai-Salam** | Full advance payment for goods delivered later (agriculture). | Trade |
| **Istisna** | An order to manufacture or construct, with staged payment. | Trade |
| **Ijarah** | Leasing (the bank owns the asset and the client pays rent). | Lease |
| **HPSM / Ijarah Muntahia Bittamleek** | A lease ending in ownership transfer. | Lease |
| **Al-Wadiah** | Safe-keeping deposit; the current account. | Deposit |
| **Qard-e-Hasan** | Benevolent loan with no profit. | Welfare |
| **Sukuk** | Asset-backed Islamic certificate (BD's first sovereign sukuk was in 2020). | Investment |

- **Prohibited:** *riba* (interest), *gharar* (excessive uncertainty) and *maysir* (gambling).
- **Firsts:** the first Islamic bank in Bangladesh was **Islami Bank Bangladesh Ltd (1983)**. **Sammilito Islami Bank PLC** (2026) is the state-owned merger of five Shariah banks: Exim, First Security, Social Islami, Union and Global Islami. Its paid-up capital is Tk 35,000 crore (government Tk 20,000 crore + depositors' shares Tk 15,000 crore), with authorised capital of Tk 40,000 crore.
