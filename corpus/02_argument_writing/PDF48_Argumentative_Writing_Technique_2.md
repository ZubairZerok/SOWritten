The price hike of essential commodities in Bangladesh has caused significant financial strain on the population.
While multiple factors contribute to this issue, it is crucial to recognize that fraudulent business syndicates bear
the exclusive responsibility for driving up prices. Their manipulative practices, market control, and exploitative
tactics have a direct impact on the affordability of basic necessities, adversely affecting the welfare of
Bangladeshi citizens. This argument will shed light on the key reasons for the price hike of essential commodities
in Bangladesh.
Fraudulent business syndicates have the ability to manipulate the supply and demand of essential commodities,
allowing them to control prices. By colluding with suppliers, these syndicates artificially limit the availability of
goods in the market, creating an artificial scarcity. This scarcity drives up prices as consumers are forced to pay
inflated amounts for basic necessities. Such manipulative tactics highlight the detrimental impact these syndicates
have on the affordability of essential commodities.

Another tactic employed by fraudulent business syndicates is hoarding essential commodities. They stockpile
large quantities of goods and create an artificial shortage, leading to an imbalance between supply and demand.
As a result, prices surge, and consumers suffer the consequences. Moreover, these syndicates engage in
speculative practices, driving up prices further by taking advantage of market fluctuations and consumer
desperation. The actions of these unscrupulous entities exacerbate the price hike problem, causing financial
hardship for ordinary citizens.
Fraudulent business syndicates take advantage of the vulnerabilities within the supply chains of essential
commodities. They exploit loopholes, bribe officials, and engage in illegal practices to gain control over the
distribution network. Through their influence, they disrupt the smooth flow of goods, leading to supply shortages
and increased prices. Their exploitative tactics contribute significantly to the price hike problem.
The price hike of essential commodities in Bangladesh cannot be solely attributed to fraudulent business
syndicates. Various external factors, such as global market trends, international trade policies, and natural
disasters, also influence the prices of goods. As Bangladesh heavily depended on importing, fluctuating
commodity prices on the global market can significantly impact the cost of imports, leading to price increases
that are beyond the control of local syndicates.
The inefficiencies within Bangladesh's distribution and storage systems contribute to the price hike issue.
Inadequate infrastructure, lack of proper storage facilities, and inefficient logistics increase the costs associated
with transporting essential commodities. These factors are also responsible for the inflated prices faced by
consumers.
In conclusion, fraudulent business syndicates are the main culprits to contribute the price hike of essential
commodities in Bangladesh, while some external factors are also responsible for this. Their manipulation of
supply and demand, hoarding practices and the absence of adequate regulatory measures all contribute to this
issue. To address the problem effectively, the government must strengthen regulatory frameworks, enhance
market oversight, and impose strict penalties on those involved in fraudulent practices.
